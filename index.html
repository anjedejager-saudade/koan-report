Email

Mailchimp · Kōan Group audience

The biggest distribution channel Kōan has. Twelve campaigns to a list of lapsed CRM contacts that had barely been mailed before. Opening is not the problem. Clicking is.

The previous period is essentially a standing start — a handful of sends — so the percentage moves on volume mean little. The two rates are the real signal.

Read the click rate carefully before it gets reported as a failure. A 58% open rate on a re-activated list is strong, and part of it is Apple's privacy proxy inflating opens. But 6,912 people opened and only 214 clicked. The subject lines and the sender are working; the offer inside the email is not pulling hard enough. That is a fixable, specific problem — and the lead magnets are the intended fix, because they ask far less of a cold contact than a webinar registration does.

Bounce at 2.1% and unsubscribes at 1.2% are both flagged amber by Mailchimp. For a list of contacts who have not heard from Kōan in a long time, that is the expected cost of waking it up, and it is cleaning itself as it goes. It becomes a real concern only if it stays there once the list is warm.

Website & search

GA4 · Google Search Console

Read this section knowing that GA4 is only seeing part of the picture. Most of what email and LinkedIn send traffic to — Mailchimp landing pages, WebinarGeek registrations, Canary — sits outside GA4 entirely.

New visitors per month

Where those 196 first arrived from

New visitors are rising steadily — 41 in June, 55 in July, 75 in August — which is the trend line to keep. One anomaly is excluded from any reading: 3 June recorded 99 active users but only 3 new ones, almost certainly a single non-human burst.

The attribution problem, stated plainly. Only 3 people are recorded as arriving first via organic social, against 119 social sessions — LinkedIn's in-app browser routinely strips the referrer, so a large share of that "Direct 148" is LinkedIn wearing a disguise. Email is worse: it is the biggest distribution channel, and it mostly points at pages GA4 never sees. The fix is UTM tags on every LinkedIn and outreach link from now on, so period 02 can report where traffic actually comes from instead of estimating it.

04

Canary & the lead magnets

PostHog · Mailchimp landing pages

Canary is Kōan's own tool: upload a report, and it assesses how well that report actually communicates. It is the sharpest qualifying signal in the funnel — nobody uploads a sustainability report out of idle curiosity.

Canary, last 90 days

What Canary tells us

603 sessions, 86 reports uploaded, 39 leads. Of everyone who uploaded and got an assessment, 45% left an email address — for a cold tool that is a very good number.

The loss is at the gate: 92 people saw an assessment and 53 of them walked away rather than give an address. That is the biggest single leak in the funnel and the cheapest one to test.

Traffic is Dutch first (142 sessions), then US (84) and UK (66). Desktop converts at 16%, mobile at 6% — this is a desk tool, and it should be promoted as one.

PostHog's window here is the last 90 days rather than the full engagement, and Canary's own funnel records 92 assessments against 86 uploads — a counting quirk when a result page is reloaded. Treat these as directional.

05

Priorities from here

Four months in, the top of the funnel is solved and the middle is not. Five priorities for the rest of the year, in the order they matter.

Priority 01

Fill the rest of the webinar calendar

The webinars are the most likely route to paid work, and the distribution behind them is already built: LinkedIn for reach, email for the invitation, and over time the website. "Biodiversity in Plain English" on 10 September is the first real test — its registration and attendance numbers become the headline of the next report.

Priority 02

A lead magnet every other week

The gated pages convert above 11% on almost no traffic. Producing one every two weeks and pushing it on LinkedIn gives the cold list something low-commitment to say yes to, which is how it gets warm enough to accept a webinar invitation later.

Priority 03

Turn LinkedIn reach into clicks

576,000 impressions and 119 social sessions. The observational posts earn the attention and spend it entirely inside LinkedIn. Give one post a week an actual destination — a lead magnet or Canary — and tag every link so the next report can prove what it did.

Priority 04

Fix the click, not the open

6,912 opens produced 214 clicks. One link, one ask, above the fold. If clicks per open moves from 3.1% toward 8%, that is roughly 340 extra people reaching a landing page for no extra sends — on the biggest channel Kōan has.

Priority 05

Grow search, and overall site traffic

Q4 work. The summer dip should reverse on its own; what it won't do is lift position on its own. Pillar content aimed at the questions this audience actually searches, plus the lead magnets and Canary as landing points, is what moves both search and total traffic.

Underneath all five

Tag everything

UTM links on every LinkedIn post and every outreach email, starting now. Right now the report can describe what happened but not reliably say which channel caused it. One month of tagged links fixes that permanently.

Where this stands commercially

Calls are happening — several over the past few weeks and another booked this week — but none has converted to paid work yet. That is the honest position at four months, and it is not unusual for a market where the buying trigger is a regulatory deadline rather than a campaign.

The working assumption from here: webinars are the number one route to paid work, with distribution coming from LinkedIn and email now, and the website as it grows. Everything in the priorities above either fills a webinar or warms the list that gets invited to one.
